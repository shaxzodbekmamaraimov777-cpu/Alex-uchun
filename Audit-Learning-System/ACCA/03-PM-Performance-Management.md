# PM — Performance Management (ACCA Applied Skills)

> PM builds on MA. It's about using management-accounting information to *manage and
> improve* business performance — costing for decisions, budgeting, variances, and
> performance measurement.

**ACCA level:** Applied Skills · **Exam:** session-based (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec), 3 hours, 50% to pass.

---

## Why this matters for your audit job

PM deepens your grip on **management estimates, cost data, and KPIs** — areas where
clients exercise judgement and where misstatement risk is higher. Strong variance and
ratio skills also power your **analytical procedures** during an audit.

---

## 1. Specialist cost & management accounting techniques

- **Activity-Based Costing (ABC)** — assign overheads via cost drivers; compare with traditional absorption costing and explain why product costs differ.
- **Target costing** — start from the market price, subtract required profit, derive the *allowable cost*, then drive cost down to meet it. Common in manufacturing.
  ```
  Target cost = Target selling price − Required profit margin
  Cost gap = Estimated cost − Target cost
  ```
- **Life-cycle costing** — total cost over a product's whole life (R&D → decline), not just the production phase.
- **Throughput accounting** — based on Theory of Constraints; maximise throughput through the bottleneck.
  ```
  Throughput = Sales − Direct material cost
  Throughput accounting ratio = Throughput per hour ÷ Factory cost per hour
  ```
- **Environmental management accounting** — identifying and controlling environmental costs.

---

## 2. Decision-making techniques

- **Relevant costing** — only **future, incremental, cash** flows are relevant. Ignore sunk costs and committed costs. Include **opportunity cost**.
- **Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) analysis** — break-even, margin of safety, target profit, the break-even and profit-volume charts.
- **Limiting factor analysis** — single scarce resource: rank by contribution per unit of scarce resource. Multiple constraints → **linear programming** (graphical method, shadow prices).
- **Pricing decisions** — cost-plus, penetration, skimming, price elasticity; the profit-maximising price where `MR = MC`.
- **Make-or-buy, shutdown, one-off contract decisions** — apply relevant costing.
- **Dealing with risk & uncertainty** — expected values, payoff tables, maximax/maximin/minimax regret, decision trees, sensitivity analysis, value of perfect information.

---

## 3. Budgeting & control

- **Budget types:** incremental, **zero-based (ZBB)**, rolling, flexible, activity-based.
- **Budgetary systems** and behavioural aspects (motivation, budget slack, participation).
- **Quantitative analysis in budgeting:** high-low, **learning curve** effects.
  ```
  Learning curve: Y = a × x^b      where b = log(learning rate) ÷ log 2
  (Y = cumulative average time per unit; x = cumulative units)
  ```
- **Standard costing** — setting standards (ideal vs attainable), and using them for control.

---

## 4. Variance analysis (the heart of PM)

**Basic variances:**
```
Materials price   = (Std price − Actual price) × Actual quantity purchased
Materials usage   = (Std qty for actual output − Actual qty) × Std price
Labour rate       = (Std rate − Actual rate) × Actual hours paid
Labour efficiency = (Std hrs for actual output − Actual hrs worked) × Std rate
Variable o/h exp. = (Std rate − Actual rate) × Actual hours
Variable o/h eff. = (Std hrs − Actual hrs) × Std rate
Fixed o/h expend. = Budgeted fixed o/h − Actual fixed o/h
Sales price       = (Actual price − Std price) × Actual units sold
Sales volume      = (Actual units − Budget units) × Std contribution (or margin)
```

**Advanced (PM-specific) variances:**
- **Materials mix and yield** — when materials can be substituted.
- **Sales mix and quantity** — when selling multiple products.
- **Planning vs operational variances** — split a variance into the part due to a bad original plan (planning) vs actual performance (operational). A key PM exam skill.

**Operating statement:** reconcile budgeted profit to actual profit through all the variances. Then *interpret* — explain the likely business reasons and their links (e.g. cheaper material → favourable price but adverse usage from poor quality).

## 5. Performance measurement & control

- **Financial performance indicators:** profitability (ROCE, margins), liquidity, gearing, efficiency ratios.
- **Non-financial indicators (NFPIs):** quality, customer satisfaction, delivery time, staff turnover.
- **The Balanced Scorecard** — four perspectives: financial, customer, internal process, learning & growth.
- **Building Block model** (Fitzgerald & Moon) — for service businesses: dimensions, standards, rewards.
- **Divisional performance:** ROI vs **Residual Income (RI)**.
  ```
  ROI = Controllable profit ÷ Controllable capital employed
  RI  = Controllable profit − (Capital employed × cost of capital)
  ```
- **Transfer pricing** — setting internal prices between divisions to motivate goal-congruent behaviour.
- **Performance in not-for-profit / public sector** — value for money: economy, efficiency, effectiveness (the "3 Es").

## 6. How auditors use PM

| PM concept | On the audit |
|------------|--------------|
| Ratio & variance analysis | Core of **analytical procedures** (expectation vs actual). |
| ROCE, margins, gearing | Spotting unusual movements that need investigation. |
| Budget vs actual | Understanding the business and identifying risk. |
| Performance KPIs | Assessing management bias in reported figures. |
| Going-concern indicators | Falling margins/liquidity feed the going-concern review. |

## 7. Self-test questions

1. A product has a target selling price of 80 and a required margin of 25%. Estimated cost is 65. What is the cost gap?
2. Distinguish a planning variance from an operational variance.
3. Division A has profit of 200,000, capital employed 1,000,000, and cost of capital 12%. Calculate ROI and Residual Income.
4. Why might a favourable material price variance cause an adverse material usage variance?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Target cost = 80 × (1 − 0.25) = 60. Cost gap = 65 − 60 = **5**.
2. Planning variance = difference caused by an unrealistic original standard (revise standard to a realistic one). Operational variance = difference between the revised realistic standard and actual performance — what management could actually control.
3. ROI = 200,000 ÷ 1,000,000 = **20%**. RI = 200,000 − (1,000,000 × 12%) = 200,000 − 120,000 = **80,000**.
4. Buying cheaper (favourable price) material may mean lower quality, leading to more waste/rework (adverse usage).
</details>
