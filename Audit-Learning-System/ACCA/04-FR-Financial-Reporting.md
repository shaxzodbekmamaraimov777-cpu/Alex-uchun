# FR — Financial Reporting (ACCA Applied Skills)

> FR teaches you the **rules** (IFRS Standards) that companies must follow when they
> prepare published financial statements. As an auditor, these rules are your benchmark:
> you check the accounts comply with them.

**ACCA level:** Applied Skills · **Exam:** session-based (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec), 3 hours, 50% to pass.

---

## Why this matters for your audit job

The whole point of an audit is forming an opinion on whether the financial statements
are prepared, in all material respects, **in accordance with the applicable financial
reporting framework** — usually **IFRS**. FR *is* that framework. When you test a
balance, you're really asking: "Does this comply with the relevant IFRS standard?"

---

## 1. The conceptual framework

The IASB's *Conceptual Framework* underpins all standards:
- **Objective of financial reporting:** provide useful information to investors, lenders, and creditors.
- **Qualitative characteristics:** *fundamental* — relevance, faithful representation; *enhancing* — comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability.
- **Elements:** assets, liabilities, equity, income, expenses.
- **Recognition & measurement:** when to put an item in the accounts and at what value (historical cost, fair value, value in use, current cost).
- **Underlying assumption:** going concern.
- **Substance over form** — account for the economic reality, not just the legal form.

## 2. Key IFRS standards (know what each one covers)

This is the most exam- *and* job-critical part. Learn the **principle and core rule** of each.

### Revenue — IFRS 15
The **5-step model** for recognising revenue:
1. Identify the contract.
2. Identify the performance obligations.
3. Determine the transaction price.
4. Allocate the price to the obligations.
5. Recognise revenue as each obligation is satisfied (at a point in time or over time).

> Revenue is the #1 audit risk area — IFRS 15 is essential.

### Property, Plant & Equipment — IAS 16
- Initially measured at **cost** (purchase price + directly attributable costs to bring it into use).
- Then either **cost model** (cost − accumulated depreciation) or **revaluation model** (fair value, with gains to a revaluation surplus).
- Depreciate over useful life; review residual value and useful life annually.

### Inventories — IAS 2
- Value at the **lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV)**.
- Cost = purchase + conversion costs (incl. production overheads). FIFO or weighted average (LIFO is **not** allowed).

### Leases — IFRS 16
- Lessees recognise almost all leases on the balance sheet: a **right-of-use asset** and a **lease liability** (present value of lease payments).
- Exemptions for short-term and low-value leases.

### Impairment — IAS 36
- An asset is impaired if **carrying amount > recoverable amount**.
- Recoverable amount = higher of *fair value less costs to sell* and *value in use* (PV of future cash flows).

### Intangibles — IAS 38
- Recognise if identifiable, controlled, and future benefits are probable.
- **Research** costs → expensed. **Development** costs → capitalised if strict criteria met.

### Provisions, contingencies — IAS 37
- **Provision:** recognise if there's a present obligation from a past event, probable outflow, and a reliable estimate.
- **Contingent liability:** disclose (don't recognise) if only possible.
- **Contingent asset:** disclose only if probable; never recognise unless virtually certain.

### Other frequently tested standards
- **IAS 12 Income taxes** — current + **deferred tax** (temporary differences).
- **IAS 10 Events after the reporting period** — adjusting vs non-adjusting events.
- **IAS 7 Statement of cash flows** — preparation, operating/investing/financing.
- **IAS 8** — accounting policies, estimates, and prior-period errors.
- **IFRS 9 Financial instruments** — classification, measurement, expected credit losses.
- **IAS 21 Foreign currency**, **IAS 23 Borrowing costs**, **IAS 20 Government grants**, **IAS 40 Investment property**, **IAS 41 Agriculture**, **IFRS 5 Non-current assets held for sale**.

## 3. Preparing single-company financial statements

You must be able to prepare, from a trial balance plus adjustments:
- Statement of Profit or Loss and Other Comprehensive Income
- Statement of Financial Position
- Statement of Changes in Equity
- Statement of Cash Flows (IAS 7)

…incorporating the adjustments above (depreciation, revaluation, tax, provisions, etc.).

---

## 4. Group accounts (consolidations)

A major FR topic. When one company **controls** another (>50% of voting rights), it
prepares **consolidated** (group) accounts.

Key mechanics:
- **Goodwill** = consideration transferred + non-controlling interest (NCI) + fair value of previously held interest − fair value of net assets acquired.
- **Non-controlling interest (NCI)** — the share of a subsidiary not owned by the parent.
- **Intra-group eliminations** — remove intra-group sales, balances, and unrealised profit in inventory.
- **Consolidated SOFP and SOPL** — add the parent and subsidiary line by line (after adjustments).
- **Associates** (significant influence, ~20–50%) — equity method.

> Consolidations are heavily examined and very relevant if HLB audits group clients.

## 5. Interpreting financial statements (ratio analysis)

| Category | Ratios |
|----------|--------|
| **Profitability** | Gross/operating/net margin, ROCE = Operating profit ÷ Capital employed |
| **Liquidity** | Current ratio = CA ÷ CL; Quick ratio = (CA − inventory) ÷ CL |
| **Efficiency** | Inventory days, receivables days, payables days, asset turnover |
| **Gearing** | Debt ÷ Equity; interest cover = Operating profit ÷ Finance costs |
| **Investor** | EPS, P/E, dividend cover |

The skill is **interpretation**: explain *why* a ratio moved and what it means for the business — exactly what you do in audit analytical procedures.

## 6. How auditors use FR

| FR concept | On the audit |
|------------|--------------|
| IFRS 15 revenue | Test recognition timing and cut-off. |
| IAS 2 inventory | Attend stock count; test lower of cost and NRV. |
| IAS 16 PPE | Verify additions, depreciation, revaluations. |
| IAS 37 provisions | Challenge whether estimates meet recognition criteria. |
| IAS 10 events after | Review for adjusting events up to the audit report date. |
| Consolidation | Audit group figures, eliminations, goodwill. |
| Ratios | Drive analytical procedures and risk assessment. |

## 7. Self-test questions

1. State the 5 steps of IFRS 15 revenue recognition.
2. At what value is inventory measured under IAS 2?
3. A building's carrying amount is 800,000; its value in use is 700,000 and fair value less costs to sell is 750,000. Is it impaired, and by how much?
4. What is the difference between a provision and a contingent liability under IAS 37?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. (1) Identify the contract; (2) identify performance obligations; (3) determine transaction price; (4) allocate price; (5) recognise revenue as obligations are satisfied.
2. The **lower of cost and net realisable value**.
3. Recoverable amount = higher of 700,000 and 750,000 = 750,000. Carrying amount 800,000 > 750,000, so impaired by **50,000**.
4. A provision is recognised (present obligation, probable outflow, reliable estimate). A contingent liability is only disclosed because the outflow is only possible or the amount can't be measured reliably.
</details>
