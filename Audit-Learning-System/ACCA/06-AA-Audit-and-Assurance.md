## 1. What is assurance and audit?

**Assurance engagement** — a professional gives an opinion that increases the confidence
of intended users about a subject matter. Five elements:
1. A **three-party relationship** (practitioner, responsible party, intended users).
2. A **subject matter** (e.g. financial statements).
3. **Suitable criteria** (e.g. IFRS).
4. Sufficient appropriate **evidence**.
5. A written **report**.

**Levels of assurance:**
- **Reasonable assurance** (high, not absolute) → positive opinion → *an audit*. "The financial statements give a true and fair view."
- **Limited assurance** (lower) → negative conclusion → *a review*. "Nothing came to our attention to suggest the statements are misstated."

**External audit objective (ISA 200):** to obtain reasonable assurance that the financial
statements as a whole are free from **material misstatement**, whether due to fraud or
error, and to report by **expressing an opinion**.

> An audit is **not** a guarantee of accuracy and is **not** designed to detect all fraud.
> This "expectation gap" is a classic exam point.

## 2. The audit process (the lifecycle of an engagement)

```
1. ACCEPTANCE & ENGAGEMENT  → assess client, independence, agree terms (engagement letter)
        ↓
2. PLANNING & RISK ASSESSMENT → understand the entity, assess risk, set materiality, strategy
        ↓
3. RISK RESPONSE / FIELDWORK   → tests of controls + substantive procedures (gather evidence)
        ↓
4. COMPLETION / REVIEW         → going concern, subsequent events, overall review, manager/partner review
        ↓
5. REPORTING                   → form the opinion, issue the auditor's report
```

You, as an intern, will spend most of your time in **stage 3 (fieldwork)** — performing
procedures and documenting them.

## 3. Audit evidence and assertions

**Audit evidence** must be **sufficient** (enough quantity) and **appropriate**
(relevant + reliable quality).

**Reliability hierarchy** (more reliable →):
- External (third party) > internal
- Auditor-obtained > client-provided
- Written > oral
- Original documents > photocopies

**Financial statement assertions** — the implicit claims management makes. You design
procedures to test each. Memorise these:

**For transactions (P&L):**
- **C**ompleteness — all transactions recorded
- **O**ccurrence — recorded transactions actually happened
- **A**ccuracy — recorded at correct amounts
- **C**ut-off — recorded in the correct period
- **C**lassification — recorded in the right accounts

**For balances (SOFP):**
- **E**xistence — assets/liabilities really exist
- **R**ights & obligations — entity owns/owes them
- **C**ompleteness — all balances recorded
- **V**aluation & allocation — at appropriate amounts

> Mnemonic for the big ones: **"ACCA ROVE"** — *Accuracy, Completeness, Classification,
> Cut-off, Occurrence + Rights, Occurrence/Existence, Valuation, Existence.* Many tutors
> simply teach **completeness, existence, rights & obligations, valuation, accuracy,
> cut-off, classification, occurrence.**

## 4. Types of audit procedures (memory aid: "AEIOU")

| Procedure | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **A**nalytical procedures | Compare/relate data, expectation vs actual | Compare this year's gross margin to last year |
| **E**nquiry & confirmation | Ask people; get third-party confirmation | Bank confirmation letter, receivables circularisation |
| **I**nspection | Examine records/documents/assets | Inspect a sales invoice; inspect a vehicle |
| **O**bservation | Watch a process being performed | Observe the inventory count |
| **R**ecalculation / **R**eperformance | Check arithmetic / redo a control | Recompute depreciation |

(Some tutors use **AEIOU**: Analytical, Enquiry, Inspection, Observation, recalcUlation.)

## 5. Audit risk model

This drives the *whole* audit. Learn it cold:

```
AUDIT RISK = INHERENT RISK × CONTROL RISK × DETECTION RISK
```

- **Inherent risk** — susceptibility to misstatement before controls (e.g. complex estimates, cash-heavy business).
- **Control risk** — risk the client's internal controls won't prevent/detect a misstatement.
- **Detection risk** — risk the auditor's procedures won't catch a misstatement. *This is the only one the auditor controls* — by doing more/better testing.

**Logic:** higher inherent + control risk → auditor must reduce detection risk → do **more** substantive work.

---

## 6. Materiality

A misstatement is **material** if it could reasonably influence users' decisions.
- Set at planning (often a % benchmark, e.g. ~0.5–1% of revenue, ~5% of profit before tax, ~1–2% of total assets — *firm guidance varies*).
- **Performance materiality** — a lower figure used during testing to leave a margin for undetected errors.
- Materiality has a **quantitative** (size) and **qualitative** (nature) dimension — e.g. a small fraud or a related-party transaction can be material by nature regardless of size.

## 7. Internal control

**Components (COSO framework):**
1. Control **environment** (the "tone at the top")
2. Risk **assessment** process
3. **Control activities** (authorisation, segregation of duties, reconciliations, physical controls)
4. **Information & communication** systems
5. **Monitoring** of controls

**Segregation of duties** — no single person should control a whole transaction
(authorise, record, and custody should be split). A core control idea.

The auditor **documents** the system (narrative notes, flowcharts, internal control
questionnaires) and may **test controls** if planning to rely on them. Control
deficiencies are reported to management in a **management letter**.

## 8. Working papers / documentation (your daily deliverable)

ISA 230 requires documentation that an **experienced auditor with no prior connection**
could understand:
- The nature, timing, and extent of procedures performed.
- The results and evidence obtained.
- Significant matters and conclusions.

A good working paper has a **header** (client, year-end, preparer, date, reviewer),
a clear **objective**, the **work done**, **results**, and a **conclusion**.
Use **tick marks** and **cross-references** to link to evidence.

> As an intern, the quality of your working papers is how seniors judge you. Be neat,
> clear, referenced, and conclusive. "What did you do, what did you find, what do you
> conclude?" — answer those three every time.

## 9. Audit sampling

You can't test everything, so you sample. ISA 530.
- **Statistical** (random, uses probability) vs **non-statistical** (judgemental) sampling.
- Methods: random, systematic, monetary unit sampling (MUS), haphazard.
- Project errors found in the sample to the whole population, then evaluate.

## 10. Substantive procedures by area (practical examples)

| Balance | Key risk | Example procedures |
|---------|----------|--------------------|
| **Receivables** | Existence, valuation | Circularise customers; review after-date cash receipts; review aged receivables for bad debts |
| **Inventory** | Existence, valuation | Attend stock count; test count vs records; check lower of cost and NRV |
| **Payables** | Completeness | Supplier statement reconciliations; search for unrecorded liabilities |
| **Bank** | Existence, completeness | Bank confirmation letter; bank reconciliation review |
| **Non-current assets** | Existence, valuation | Inspect assets; verify additions to invoices; recompute depreciation |
| **Revenue** | Occurrence, cut-off | Test sales around year-end; trace to dispatch; analytical review of margins |

## 11. Going concern (ISA 570)

The assumption the business will continue for the foreseeable future (~12 months).
Indicators of doubt: net liabilities, recurring losses, loss of key customer, inability
to pay debts, breached loan covenants. The auditor evaluates management's assessment and
considers the impact on the audit report.

## 12. The auditor's report and opinions

**Unmodified opinion** — financial statements give a **true and fair view** ("present fairly"). The clean result.

**Modified opinions** — when there's a problem:

| Situation | If material but **not** pervasive | If material **and** pervasive |
|-----------|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Misstatement** (financial statements are wrong) | **Qualified** ("except for") | **Adverse** (statements do not give a true and fair view) |
| **Inability to obtain evidence** (scope limitation) | **Qualified** ("except for") | **Disclaimer** (we do not express an opinion) |

Other report elements:
- **Material uncertainty related to going concern** paragraph.
- **Emphasis of Matter** — draws attention to something properly disclosed (doesn't modify the opinion).
- **Other Matter** paragraph.
- **Key Audit Matters (KAM)** — for listed entities, the most significant areas.

## 13. Professional ethics & independence (ISA / IESBA Code)

Five **fundamental principles** (mnemonic **"PICCO"**):
- **P**rofessional behaviour
- **I**ntegrity
- **C**onfidentiality
- **C**ompetence & due care (professional)
- **O**bjectivity

**Threats to independence:** self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity,
intimidation. Apply **safeguards** (rotation, separate teams, declining the work).

> Independence is sacred in audit. Never accept gifts, never audit your own work, never
> develop too-close relationships that compromise objectivity. If unsure — ask.

---

## 14. Fraud, laws & regulations

- **ISA 240** — fraud. Management is primarily responsible for prevention; the auditor
  maintains **professional scepticism** and considers fraud risk (especially revenue
  recognition and management override of controls).
- **ISA 250** — consider laws and regulations affecting the financial statements.
- **Professional scepticism** — a questioning mind; don't take management's word at face value.

## 16. Self-test questions

1. What are the three components of the audit risk model?
2. Give the two conditions for an audit opinion to be (a) qualified, (b) adverse, (c) disclaimer.
3. Rank these from most to least reliable: oral management enquiry; bank confirmation letter; client-prepared schedule; auditor's own recalculation.
4. Name the five fundamental ethical principles.
5. What's the difference between reasonable and limited assurance?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Inherent risk, control risk, detection risk.
2. (a) Qualified — material but not pervasive misstatement, OR material but not pervasive inability to obtain evidence. (b) Adverse — material **and** pervasive misstatement. (c) Disclaimer — material **and** pervasive inability to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence.
3. Most → least: bank confirmation letter (external, written) > auditor's own recalculation > client-prepared schedule > oral management enquiry.
4. Professional behaviour, Integrity, Confidentiality, Professional competence & due care, Objectivity (PICCO).
5. Reasonable assurance is high (but not absolute) and gives a positive opinion (audit). Limited assurance is lower and gives a negative-form conclusion (review): "nothing came to our attention…".
</details>
