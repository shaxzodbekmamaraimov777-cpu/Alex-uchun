# FM — Financial Management (ACCA Applied Skills)

> FM is about how a business **manages its money**: making investment decisions, managing
> cash and working capital, raising finance, and understanding value and risk.

**ACCA level:** Applied Skills · **Exam:** session-based (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec), 3 hours, 50% to pass.

---

## Why this matters for your audit job

FM gives you the tools to assess a client's **financial health, cash position, and
going-concern status** — and to understand how they value assets and investments.
Working-capital and ratio skills feed directly into audit risk assessment.

---

## 1. The financial management function

- **Goal:** maximise shareholder wealth (long-term value), balancing risk and return.
- **Three key decisions:** investment, financing, and dividend.
- **Stakeholders & agency theory** — the conflict between managers (agents) and owners (principals), and how to align them (incentives, governance).
- **Financial objectives** vs non-financial; the role of financial markets.

---

## 2. Working capital management

Working capital = **current assets − current liabilities**. Managing it well keeps the
business liquid. This is one of the most practically useful FM areas for audit.

**The cash operating cycle** (the gap between paying suppliers and collecting from customers):
```
Cash cycle = Inventory days + Receivables days − Payables days
```
- **Inventory days** = (Inventory ÷ Cost of sales) × 365
- **Receivables days** = (Receivables ÷ Credit sales) × 365
- **Payables days** = (Payables ÷ Credit purchases) × 365

**Managing each element:**
- **Inventory:** Economic Order Quantity (EOQ), reorder levels, JIT.
  ```
  EOQ = √( (2 × annual demand × order cost) ÷ holding cost per unit )
  ```
- **Receivables:** credit policy, early-settlement discounts, factoring, invoice discounting.
- **Payables:** take credit, but evaluate the cost of losing early-payment discounts.
- **Cash:** cash budgets, Baumol and Miller-Orr models for optimal cash balances.

---

## 3. Investment appraisal (capital budgeting)

How to decide whether a long-term project is worthwhile.

| Method | Rule | Notes |
|--------|------|-------|
| **Payback period** | Shorter = better | Ignores time value of money & cash after payback |
| **Accounting Rate of Return (ARR)** | Higher = better | Uses profits, not cash |
| **Net Present Value (NPV)** | Accept if NPV > 0 | **The best method** — uses discounted cash flows |
| **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** | Accept if IRR > cost of capital | Discount rate where NPV = 0 |

**Discounting (time value of money):**
```
Present value = Future cash flow ÷ (1 + r)^n
```
NPV = sum of all discounted cash flows − initial investment.

**Advanced points:** relevant cash flows only, tax and **capital allowances (tax depreciation)**,
inflation (money vs real rates), working-capital changes, and dealing with risk
(sensitivity analysis, probabilities).

## 4. Sources of finance

- **Equity:** ordinary shares (rights issues, IPOs), retained earnings.
- **Debt:** bank loans, bonds/loan notes, leasing, convertibles.
- **Short-term:** overdraft, trade credit, invoice finance.
- **For small/growing firms:** venture capital, business angels, crowdfunding, government grants.
- **Islamic finance** basics (relevant in Uzbekistan/Central Asia): *murabaha, ijara, mudaraba, musharaka, sukuk* — finance structured to comply with Sharia (no interest/riba).

**Capital structure & cost of capital:**
- **Cost of equity** — via the Dividend Growth Model or **CAPM**: `Ke = Rf + β(Rm − Rf)`.
- **Cost of debt** — after-tax cost (interest is tax-deductible).
- **WACC** (Weighted Average Cost of Capital) — blended cost used as the discount rate:
  ```
  WACC = (E/V × Ke) + (D/V × Kd × (1 − tax rate))
  ```

---

## 5. Business & security valuation

- **Asset-based** valuation (net asset value).
- **Income-based:** P/E ratio method, earnings yield.
- **Cash-flow-based:** dividend valuation model, discounted cash flow.
- **Efficient Market Hypothesis** — weak, semi-strong, strong forms.

---

## 6. Risk management

- **Foreign exchange risk** — transaction, translation, economic risk. Hedging with forwards, money-market hedges, futures, options.
- **Interest-rate risk** — forward rate agreements, swaps, options.
- The principle: reduce uncertainty in cash flows to protect value.

## 7. How auditors use FM

| FM concept | On the audit |
|------------|--------------|
| Working-capital ratios | Liquidity & going-concern assessment. |
| Cash cycle / cash budgets | Evidence the client can pay its debts. |
| NPV / impairment cash flows | Audit value-in-use calculations (links to IAS 36). |
| Gearing & interest cover | Assess financial risk and covenant compliance. |
| FX exposure | Audit foreign-currency balances and hedging. |
| Cost of capital | Sense-check discount rates in client models. |

## 8. Self-test questions

1. A company has inventory days of 60, receivables days of 45, and payables days of 30. What is its cash operating cycle?
2. A project costs 100,000 now and returns 60,000 in year 1 and 70,000 in year 2. At a 10% discount rate, what is the NPV? (Discount factors: yr1 0.909, yr2 0.826.)
3. Why is NPV considered superior to payback period?
4. State the WACC formula in words.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. 60 + 45 − 30 = **75 days**.
2. PV = (60,000 × 0.909) + (70,000 × 0.826) = 54,540 + 57,820 = 112,360. NPV = 112,360 − 100,000 = **+12,360** (accept the project).
3. NPV accounts for the time value of money and all cash flows over the project's life, and gives an absolute measure of value added; payback ignores both the time value of money and cash flows after the payback point.
4. WACC = (proportion of equity × cost of equity) + (proportion of debt × after-tax cost of debt).
</details>
